Brief 003 · Recording screen · style decision

Montage head styles

These are four ways the Recording screen's montage view could look. Each sketch is live: drag to rotate, click an electrode or a channel below to select it. All four use the bundled example's 16 channels at their standard 10-10 positions. These are browser sketches of the look. The app would rebuild the chosen one natively, and the intro's scalp map would use the same style.

Channels

A · Glass dome

Recommended
drag to rotate

A see-through scalp cap with no face. The full 10-10 grid is drawn faintly across it, and your channels sit on it as flush discs. The back of the grid shows through the glass.

Good
Matches the app's glass look. Reads as an instrument, not a mannequin. Back electrodes stay visible.
Watch
Abstract: the only cues for "front" are the nose notch and the grid.
Brief
No change.

B · Sculpted head

Brief unchanged
drag to rotate

One smooth, sculpted porcelain head (brow, nose, chin, ears and neck blended into a single surface), with studio lighting and metal cup electrodes.

Good
Instantly readable orientation. Feels physical, like a lab mannequin.
Watch
Hides the back of the head. A near-real face can look off, and it's the most work to polish natively.
Brief
No change.

C · Contour hologram

Brief unchanged
drag to rotate

The same head shape as B, drawn only as fine contour lines and a glowing edge, with glowing electrodes. It's the closest match to the intro's "neural field" motion.

Good
Striking, and ties the Recording screen to the intro. The lines show the shape without a solid face.
Watch
The most decorative of the four, and weakest in light mode.
Brief
No change.

D · Flat top-down map

Reverses #18
No rotation: it's always the top view, with the nose up and your right on the right.

The standard diagram EEG tools print: head circle, nose, ears, electrodes laid out flat.

Good
Most legible and familiar to researchers. Quickest to build. Nothing ever hides behind the head.
Watch
Not 3D: it drops drag-to-rotate and the view buttons (decision #18). Low-hanging sites like T9 fall outside the circle.
Brief
Replaces the 3D criteria with a 2D map.

Not sketched here

A professionally modeled head file (licensed or commissioned) could look better than B. It breaks the brief's "built from code, no model files" rule, and it may cost money or need a license check, so there's nothing to show until you want to go that way. A and B could also be combined: B's head for orientation, with A's glass cap over the scalp.

Electrode positions: the 10-10 system, Oostenveld & Praamstra (2001), the same table the app uses. Channels: the bundled PhysioNet EEGMMIDB S001 example. These sketches are drawn in the browser and are illustrations of the look, not screenshots of the app.
